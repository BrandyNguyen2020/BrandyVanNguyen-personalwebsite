The Southern California Coastal Waters Research Project is a joint-appointed agency by sevearl city counties around Southern California to address a key need for rigorous science and research to support water management, quality, and assessment in the region. As such, SCCWRP has had a long-standing partnership with many state-agencies across California, where one of our key roles is to provide the science needed to make informed management decisions. More specifically, I work as a lab assistant in the Biogeochemistry Department. For the most part, I assist projects related to improving and monitoring ways in which we can track, respond, and understand the effects of Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs) and ocean acidification (OA) in the Southern California Bight and freshwater systems in the state. Some more indepth project details below!

<div class="caption">
    Pteropods are a type of pelagic sea snail (sea angels!), and they serve as a cool model organism to assess the effect of ocean acidification via their shell dissolution. One project I work on at SCCWRP is assisting in creating a standerdized protocol for assessing what shell dissolution looks like based on a grading system. Pteropods are still a relatively new model organism to track ocean acidification, and as such SCCWRP has been working with collaborators from UC San Diego and the Northwest Fisheries Science Center to develop this protocol. A Standard Electron Microscope is used to capture topographic images of a pteropod shell and patches of shell dissolution are than outlined to quantify the effect of OA. This project is still in development, but is cool nonetheless!

<div class="caption">
    Another project I'm a part of is monitoring for Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs) across the state. Recently, SCCWRP has taken over sampling at Newport Beach Pier, CA for the Southern California Coastal Ocean Observing System (a position previsouly held by the University of Southern California). The point of this sampling is to monitor the presence of toxic cyanobacteria and their related toxins in real-time, providing weekly information to a database compiled by SCCOOS, which has sister programs across the Northern and Central region of California as well. The images above are some of the cool plankton I've seen when assessing zooplankton tow net samples I've pulled up for this sampling-things like diatoms, an acantharian, and a larval snail!

Alright the last project doesn't really have any accompanying pictures so I'll talk about it here. The California State Water Boards Freshwater Harmful Algal Blooms Program utilizes a chart of compiled potentially toxic cyanobacteria to inform which cyanotoxins to test for in a water sample. My place in this scheme is that I conducted the literature review to compile this list of all known potentially toxic cyanobacteria! The publicaiton is soon to come, check back!
